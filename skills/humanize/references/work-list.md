# Extended word and phrase list
 
Read this when doing a review pass on existing text, or when you want a substitution for a flagged word. During normal drafting the shorter list in SKILL.md is enough.
 
Nothing here is banned. Each entry is a word that models overproduce relative to how often writers actually reach for it. The test is always the same: did this word get chosen, or did it arrive?

**Adjectives**
 
intricate, pivotal, crucial, vital, essential, seamless, robust, vibrant, nuanced, holistic, comprehensive, cutting-edge, transformative, groundbreaking, innovative, meticulous, invaluable, unwavering, profound, remarkable, rich (as in "rich history"), diverse (as filler), key (as filler)
 
Most of these are intensifiers pretending to be descriptions. `A robust solution` says nothing that `a solution that survives partial network failure` doesn't say better.
 
**Nouns**
 
tapestry, landscape (figurative), realm, arena, sphere, journey (figurative), cornerstone, testament, beacon, hallmark, plethora, myriad, array (as in "a wide array of"), insights, takeaways, synergy, ecosystem (outside biology), framework (when nothing structural is meant)
 
**Adverbs and qualifiers**
 
arguably, notably, importantly, significantly, effectively, essentially, ultimately, truly, indeed, undoubtedly, seamlessly, meticulously
 
Most adverbs in this class can be deleted without changing the sentence's meaning. That is the test: delete it, reread, see whether anything was lost.
 
## 2. Openers and connectives
 
Sentence openers that appear far more often in generated text than in edited prose:
 
- Moreover,
- Furthermore,
- Additionally,
- In addition,
- Notably,
- Importantly,
- Consequently,
- Nevertheless,
- That said,
- Ultimately,
- At its core,
- In today's [adjective] world,
- As we [verb] the [noun] of...
Replacements: but, so, and, still, yet, then, because. Or nothing. Two sentences placed next to each other usually imply their own relationship.

## 4. Sentence shapes
 
Patterns rather than words. These are harder to catch because no individual word is flagged.
 
**The negative parallel.** It's not X, it's Y. This isn't just X. It's Y. Not merely A, but B.
 
**The tricolon.** Three parallel items, especially three adjectives or three noun phrases, especially when the third adds nothing.
 
**The balanced hedge.** While X offers clear benefits, it also presents challenges. Every claim neutralized in the same sentence.
 
**The significance frame.** X represents a broader shift toward Y. X reflects growing interest in Y. X has become emblematic of Y.
 
**The rhetorical question opener.** Ever wondered what happens when...? What if there were a better way?
 
**The participial tail.** A clause hanging off the end of the sentence that summarizes what the sentence just said, effectively restating the point in shorter form. Like that one.
 
**The bolded bullet.** `**Scalability:** The system scales well across use cases.` The definition restates the label.
 
**The em-dash aside.** A sentence interrupted for emphasis where a comma would carry the same load.
 
## 5. Punctuation and typography
 
- Em dashes used for emphasis rather than genuine interruption.
- Hyphens where en dashes belong: ranges (1990–2000), scores (3–2), against/between (the Smith–Jones model).
- Curly quotes and apostrophes in contexts where the surrounding text uses straight ones, a sign text was pasted from a chat interface.
- Title Case On Every Heading, especially inconsistently.
- Emojis in headings or as bullet markers.
- Bold applied to whole sentences.
- Three or more heading levels in a document under 1000 words.
